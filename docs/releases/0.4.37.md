# Release 0.4.37 — Argentine Senate stenographic transcripts, 2000–2024

The first cut of this corpus as a citable dataset. The version is the parser
version that produced it, which is the rule this project follows: a release is
worth cutting when the corpus content changes, not when the documentation moves.

## What is in it

559 sittings of the Argentine Senate, 23 February 2000 to 12 December 2024,
parsed from the chamber's own stenographic PDFs. 558 of them parse; the one
that does not is a November 2001 sitting that never reached quorum, so it has
no session opening to find, and it is reported rather than dropped quietly.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Rows | 233,408 |
| Words | 21,457,100 |
| Speech passages | 151,761 |
| Typed stenographer's notes | 31,955 |
| Passages the parser could not attribute | 1,109 (0.5%) |
| Resolved (sitting, printed label) pairs | 16,311 |

The bundle's contents and what each part is for are listed in
[RELEASE.md](../RELEASE.md). The source PDFs are not redistributed: they are
the Senate's to publish, and `raw_data_manifest.csv` identifies every one by
checksum and URL so anyone can fetch them and rebuild this exact corpus.

## What the checks said on this build

Every number below is printed by a script in `scripts/`, and every one of them
can be re-run against the shipped data.

**Against 36 pages annotated by hand** (`eval_gold.py`): boundary and
attribution F1 = 0.996, 124 of 125 turns, the same on the printed label alone
and on the label carried with the turn's opening words. Stenographer's notes:
precision 1.000, recall 0.935 (29 of 31). Read that as the small sample it is —
one miss in 125 turns is a 95% interval on recall of 0.956 to 0.999, the turns
come from 24 documents, and two thirds of the pages are 2020 or later.

**The annotations against the source pages** (`check_gold.py`): 36 of 36. Each
annotated turn's opening words are printed on that page immediately after that
speaker's own label.

**Every sitting against its own PDF** (`audit_parse.py`): no page apparatus
inside a speech turn outside the two scanned sittings, no turn carrying a second
speaker's label, no document yielding more text than it prints, 98,601 blocks
probed with 0.120% not located and no sitting above 1% once the scans are set
aside, and a median 79.0% of each document's printed text kept — the rest is
contents pages, attendance rolls and appendices, dropped on purpose.

**5,463 turns read blind** (`check_blind_reads.py`): 5,416 still resolve to the
person the round recorded and none resolves to anybody else. 47 cannot be
re-asked and are not failures — 43 quote words the page prints under two
different names, two are all an unmapped font left of a passage, and two are
notes a repair has since moved out of speech.

**The caucus** (`resolve_speakers.py`): 97.6% of senators' floor passages carry
one. 83.2% confirmed, 13.1% marked anachronistic because the Senate's own record
names a caucus that did not exist on the day of the sitting, 1.1% undatable,
0.1% disputed between two records that could each describe the day. Those are
kept and marked, never corrected or dropped.

## What changed the corpus's content on the way here

Three parser versions moved text or changed who is credited with it.

- **0.4.35** — the stenographers' sign-off was landing inside a senator's
  question. The strip collected only the last 70 points of the page, and 18 of
  the 559 sittings print the footer a little higher. Removing it took 1,250
  furniture rows out, rejoined 753 speech rows the footer had cut in two, and
  recovered 19 stenographer's notes, across 14 sittings.
- **0.4.36** — headings with no word character in them, left by OCR noise on a
  scanned sitting, were saying a section began without being able to say which.
- **0.4.37** — "Sr. Secretario (Estrada). — (Lee:)" is one printed line: the
  secretary was given the floor and the stenographer wrote down that he read.
  The note is not speech, so it is typed as an event and events carry no
  speaker, but the label was consumed and its turn held nothing else, so 265
  printed labels were written nowhere at all. A note directly under a printed
  label now carries it — 331 rows — and no row changes type.

Two changes outside the parser also change what ships. The caucus gains a
`disputed` status for the 113 passages where two records that could each
describe the day name different caucuses. And the verification itself was
rebuilt after two rounds of adversarial review found the checks passing for the
wrong reasons; `TODO.md` Phases 32 and 33 record what was wrong with them and
what the controls now show.

## Citing it

Two things are being cited and they are not the same. The transcripts are the
Senate's; cite the chamber as publisher with the sitting date and the
per-sitting URL from the manifest. This corpus — the parsing, the speaker
resolution, the hand-dated caucuses and the verification — is the work in this
repository: the code is MIT, the derived tables and reference data are
**CC BY 4.0** ([LICENSE-DATA](../../LICENSE-DATA)). The reasoning is in
[SOURCES.md](../../SOURCES.md) and [RELEASE.md](../RELEASE.md).

> Ripamonti, J. (2026). *Argentine Senate stenographic transcripts, 2000–2024:
> a speaker-attributed corpus* (version 0.4.37) [Data set].

## Known limits

- Two sittings of November 2001 are scans read by OCR. They are flagged in
  `parse_stats.csv` and should be excluded from any text analysis.
- 91 printed speaker labels still leave no row of their own.
- A handful of genuinely printed blocks are reported by the audit as text it
  cannot find, because a repair and the PDF extractor disagree about the same
  characters; [SOURCES.md](../../SOURCES.md) works one through.
- The gold set's one miss is the secretary's turn described above: the
  annotation calls a printed label with only a note under it a turn, and the
  corpus records it without calling it speech.
