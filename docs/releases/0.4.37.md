# ar_congress 0.4.37

Speaker-attributed corpus of Argentine Senate stenographic transcripts,
February 2000 to December 2024, parsed from the chamber's own PDFs.

Released 8 September 2026. First tagged release.

## Contents

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Sittings | 559 downloaded, 558 parsed |
| Rows | 233,408 |
| Words | 21,457,100 |
| Speech passages | 151,761 |
| Stenographer's notes, typed | 31,955 |
| Passages with no speaker | 1,109 |
| Resolved (sitting, label) pairs | 16,311 |

The sitting that does not parse is 29 November 2001. It never reached quorum,
so it has no session opening for the parser to find, and it is reported in
`parse_stats.csv` rather than dropped.

`RELEASE.md` lists what the bundle holds and why each part is in it. The source
PDFs are not redistributed: `raw_data_manifest.csv` identifies each one by
checksum and URL, and `scripts/download.py` fetches them again.

## Changes

Three parser versions changed the corpus content.

**0.4.35.** The stenographers' sign-off was landing inside a senator's
question. The footer strip only looked at the last 70 points of the page, and
18 of the 559 sittings print the line higher than that. Fixing it removed 1,250
furniture rows, rejoined 753 speech rows the footer had cut in two, and
recovered 19 stenographer's notes, across 14 sittings.

**0.4.36.** Headings with no word character in them, left by OCR noise on a
scanned sitting, were recording that a section began without saying which.

**0.4.37.** "Sr. Secretario (Estrada). — (Lee:)" is one printed line: the
secretary was given the floor, and the stenographer wrote down that he read.
The note is not speech, so it is typed as an event, and events carry no
speaker. But the label was consumed like any other, and the turn it opened held
nothing else, so 265 printed labels were written nowhere at all. A note
directly under a printed label now carries it. 331 rows gained a speaker. No
row changed type.

Two changes outside the parser also affect the shipped tables. `bloc_status`
gains a fourth value, `disputed`, for the 113 passages where two records that
could each describe the day name different caucuses. And the verification
scripts were rebuilt after two rounds of adversarial review found several of
them passing for the wrong reasons; `TODO.md` phases 32 and 33 have the detail.

## Verification

Every figure below is printed by a script in `scripts/` and can be re-run
against the shipped data.

**36 hand-annotated pages** (`eval_gold.py`). Boundary and attribution
F1 = 0.996, 124 of 125 turns, the same on the printed label alone and on the
label carried with the turn's opening words. Stenographer's notes: precision
1.000, recall 0.935, 29 of 31.

Treat 0.996 as the small sample it is. One miss in 125 turns puts the 95%
interval on recall at 0.956 to 0.999, the 125 turns come from 24 documents, and
24 of the 36 pages are 2020 or later.

**The annotations against their own pages** (`check_gold.py`): 36 of 36. Each
annotated turn's opening words are printed immediately after that speaker's
label on that page.

**Every sitting against its PDF** (`audit_parse.py`):

- no page apparatus inside a speech turn, outside the two scanned sittings
- no turn carrying a second speaker's label
- no document yielding more text than it prints, 0 of 558
- 98,601 blocks probed, 0.120% not found in the source, no sitting above 1%
  once the two scans are set aside
- median 79.0% of each document's printed text kept. The rest is contents
  pages, attendance rolls and appendices, dropped on purpose.

**5,463 turns read blind** (`check_blind_reads.py`). 5,416 still resolve to the
person the round recorded, and none resolves to anybody else. 47 cannot be
re-asked: 43 quote words the page prints under two different names, 2 are all
an unmapped font left of a passage, 2 are notes a repair has since moved out of
speech.

**Caucus** (`resolve_speakers.py`). 97.6% of senators' floor passages carry
one: 83.2% confirmed, 13.1% marked anachronistic because the Senate's own
record names a caucus that did not exist on the day of the sitting, 1.1%
undatable, 0.1% disputed. Those are kept and marked, never corrected or
dropped.

## Known issues

**The 1,109 passages with no speaker are not one thing.** 362 are in the two
November 2001 scans, where the record is the OCR of a bill printed into it and
no label survives. 321 are in three sittings whose record is mostly an inserted
document: lists of judicial appointments, decree texts. Of the remaining 426,
155 are a word or less (stray full stops, a single letter, an orphan
parenthetical such as "(Guinle)", a contents-page "Volver"). At least 42 are
real floor speech that lost its label, such as "Señor presidente: voy a ser muy
breve porque hemos acordado que sea el presidente de nue…". That count comes
from matching how a turn opens, so it is a floor rather than a total.

**Two sittings of November 2001 are scans read by OCR.** They are flagged in
`parse_stats.csv` and should be excluded from text analysis.

**91 printed speaker labels still leave no row of their own.**

**The audit reports a few genuinely printed blocks as text it cannot find**,
where a parser repair and the PDF extractor disagree about the same characters.
`SOURCES.md` works one of them through.

**The gold set's one miss is the secretary's turn described above.**
`eval_gold.py` counts speech turns; the corpus records that turn as a note. The
two will keep disagreeing until somebody decides whether a printed label with
only a stenographer's note under it is a turn.

**36 notes reading "-Okay" are attributed to the chair but typed as notes.**
The page shows "Sra. Presidente.- Okay.", so she said it; the typesetter set it
in italics, which is what makes the parser call a line a note. Four sittings of
2016.

## The bundle

`ar_congress_senado_0.4.37`, 1,257 files, 96 MB unpacked, 68 MB as
`ar_congress_senado_0.4.37.tar.gz`.

It is laid out the way the repository is, so every path named in the
documentation is the path in the bundle. Besides the corpus, the reference
tables and the verification records, it carries the pipeline that produced it
(`scripts/`, with `pyproject.toml` and `uv.lock` for the pinned environment),
the 559 per-sitting parse logs, the portal listing snapshots, what the
verification scripts printed on this build, the analysis notebook with its four
figures, and the documentation — `README.md`, `SOURCES.md`, the data dictionary,
this file, and `TODO.md`, which is where the README and these notes point when
they cite a phase as the record of what was repaired. `CITATION.cff` carries the
citation in machine-readable form.

The source PDFs are the one thing not here: they are the Senate's to publish.
`raw_data_manifest.csv` identifies each by SHA-256 and URL, and the shipped
`scripts/download.py` fetches them again.

`CHECKSUMS.sha256` inside the bundle carries a SHA-256 for each of the other
1,256 files. Check them with `shasum -a 256 -c CHECKSUMS.sha256` from the
unpacked directory. The archive's own SHA-256 sits beside it in
`ar_congress_senado_0.4.37.tar.gz.sha256`, since it cannot be inside the
archive it describes.
