# ar_congress 0.5.8

Speaker-attributed corpus of Argentine Senate stenographic transcripts,
February 1998 to September 2026, parsed from the chamber's own PDFs and, for
1998–2003, from its HTML export.

Cut 25 September 2026. PDF parser 0.5.8, HTML parser 0.5.11-html. The previous
release was 0.4.37, of 8 September 2026.

The sitting that does not parse is now the impeachment tribunal of 18
December 1997, a photocopy saved as page images with no text in it. The
no-quorum sitting of 29 November 2001, which 0.4.37 could not parse, is in.

Most of the rise in unattributed passages is the HTML era, 4,407 of the
6,399: its export prints inserted
matter — speeches handed in for the record and never delivered, and the bills
read into it — inside the document, and 83% of those words stand in such a run.
The rest are the PDF era's, where 87% of the words stand in a run the page
opens with "— El texto es el siguiente:" or "…son los siguientes:", now
nobody's (below).

## Changes

`TODO.md`, phases 34 to 41, is the record of each change and the evidence for
it. In summary:

**The HTML era (phase 34).** The portal serves the same URL as a PDF or as
the chamber's HTML export depending on the sitting's age, and the downloader
had been rejecting everything that was not a PDF. Accepting both, and
reaching back before 2000 and on past 2024, added 260 files — 214 HTML exports
of 1998–2003, 17 PDFs of 1997–1999 and 29 of 2025–2026: 559 holdings became
819. A second
parser, `parse_html.py`, reads the exports into the same table, and every row
now says which format it came from (`source_format`; `source_pdf` and
`pdf_sha256` are renamed `source_file` and `source_sha256`). The cover pages
of 1998–2003 were read for who held the chair, and the two senators named
Sapag are told apart by the chamber's courtesy title, which each row settled
that way records in `tiebreak`.

**Caucuses before the rosters.** No roster of caucuses survives before May
2000. The 1998–2000 caucuses are rebuilt from the chair's own calls, which
often name the caucus when giving the floor, from senators' statements on the floor,
from archived copies of senators' own pages, and, where the chamber's official
count per caucus leaves only one answer, from that count. A caucus is carried
across a gap only where the observations on both sides agree, and is marked as
inferred. Every observation records its source.

**Labels the parser was not reading (phases 35 and 41).** The honorific as the
HTML typists spell it ("Sr.Presidente", "Sr Sager", "SR. PRESIDENTE"), labels
split across bold runs, labels ended with a full stop and no dash, labels a
printing defect had cut, and a chair's closing parenthesis left at the start
of the speech. Printed labels that open a turn with no row fell from 91 in
0.4.37 to 19.

**The sitting that held the wrong document (phase 40).** 3–4 September 2014
shipped in 0.4.37 as mostly a committee meeting of 19 August appended to the
file: the parser began reading where that meeting starts. It now reads the
sitting the file is named for, which goes from 118,986 words to 251,293.
Along with it: a file that prints its sitting twice keeps the first copy, a
repeating line counts as a running header only if it opens the page, and a
sitting's body type size may wobble by half a point.

**Notes and italics (phases 39–41).** One row per printed note; "— El texto
es el siguiente:" is the chair's words where the page sets it in the speech
and a note where it stands alone; the secretary's "(Lee:)" is his turn; a
bracketed note printed inside a speaker's sentence is part of that speech.
Italics printed straight after a label now belong to that label.

**The reviews before release (phases 42 and 43).** Two rounds of independent
reviewers read the candidate bundle, cut first as 0.5.6 and then as 0.5.7,
neither of which was published. Every finding was checked against the printed
page and scoped by a scan of the whole corpus before anything was changed.

The first round found 38 speech passages of 11 April 2002 carrying the
dateline "Pág.. N", printed with two full stops; six rows with no visible
text; and a 1998 label printed "Sr. PRESIDENTE Menem)" resolved to Ruckauf,
who held the office, instead of Menem, who held the chair. It also found that
the bundle could not fetch its own sources: `download.py --from-manifest` now
does, checking each file against its recorded hash.

The second round found words given to the wrong speaker or kept out of
speech, in five patterns, all now repaired:

- **Debate set a point smaller than the body** (2006–2014), which had gone
  out as page matter with its labels, so the words after it fell to whoever
  spoke before: 30 March 2011 gave the chair's "En consideración en general"
  to Pichetto, and 15 August 2012 had lost 3,576 words of debate. The
  no-quorum sitting of 10 November 2010, whose body size was misread, gets its
  minority speeches back.
- **HTML labels the typist broke**: a bold run split inside the name ("Sr.
  Vaqui" + "r. --"), a label run on mid-paragraph after the last speaker's
  full stop, dots or a dash in front of it, or a centred label read as a
  section title. Genoud's 1,413-word homage of 11 August 1999 had been the
  chair's.
- **Foreign heads of state** resolved to the Argentine official chairing:
  Lula (2003) to Scioli, Mattarella (2017) to Michetti.
- **Matter appended after the record's sign-off**: committee meetings bound
  into three files of 2005 (176 turns of one of them), and the speeches
  senators handed in for the record in 2005–2007, whose opening "Sr.
  Presidente:" had been read as the chair. It is appendix, as elsewhere.
- **Documents printed after "— El texto es el siguiente:"** or "— Los
  proyectos en consideración, cuyos textos se incluyen en el Apéndice, son
  los siguientes:" — bills, work plans, lists of titles — had been the words
  of whoever spoke before the note. They are now nobody's, as the HTML half
  always had them, which moves about 300,000 words of 2001–2019 out of
  speech.

Senators who chair a sitting keep their own identity and caucus: since 2020
they had resolved to an office, about 9,260 passages with no caucus. Scripts
that take no options no longer run when asked for `--help`, and the steps
that build a table from all the sources refuse to run on a partial set.

**New columns.** `convened_as` and `quorum_failed` separate how a sitting was
called from whether it reached quorum, which the chamber's single label
(`session_kind`) conflated. `tiebreak` in `speakers.parquet`, above.

## Verification

Every figure is printed by a script in `scripts/`.

**72 annotated PDF pages** (`eval_gold.py`), up from 36, spanning 1998–2024,
annotated from the page images by a language model, not by a person; the 36
new ones are all before 2016 and were each read twice, in separate runs,
against a written brief and without the parser's answer (the bundle's README
says how independent each batch is). Boundary and attribution
F1 = 1.000, 310 of 310 turns, on the label alone and with the turn's opening
words. Notes: 82 of 82. A perfect 310 still puts the 95% interval on recall at
0.988 to 1.000. One page was retired, kept with the reason: it was drawn from
the committee meeting the 2014 file appends.

**24 annotated HTML stretches** (`eval_gold_html.py`), new, annotated the
same way. About 7,000
characters each, cut on the source by offset, four per year across
1998–2003, each read twice; the readings agree on all 195 turn starts, and
both score 195 of 195, notes included.

**The annotations against their sources** (`check_gold.py`): 108 of 108.

**Every sitting against its source file** (`audit_parse.py`): no page apparatus inside a speech
turn outside the three scans, no turn carrying a second speaker's label
outside the scans (8 inside them), no
document yielding more text than it prints (0 of 817), and every one of
14,443 labels of the HTML era's commonest shape — a bold run that begins at
the heading above — attributed to its own speaker. 153,631 passages were
probed against the source and 80 could not be located (0.052%), 68 of them in
the two scans; with the scans set aside it is 12 of 153,377 (0.008%), no
sitting above 1%. 7 passages of 244,805 open in lower case under a new
speaker, each on a whole word, and every one is printed that way.
A median 82.6% of each document's printed text is kept, the rest being
contents pages, attendance rolls and appendices, dropped by design. Every page
of the 90 PDF sittings that keep least was checked against the output: what is
missing before the sitting's close is covers, rolls and contents, not debate.
The audit's split-label check now reads a misspelt office word ("Sr.
Presidented (Maqueda)") the way speaker resolution does; it had been failing
on the one such label whose parse had repaired the typo.

**6,819 turns read blind** in thirteen rounds (`check_blind_reads.py`), up
from 5,463 in nine, each by a language model reading the page without the
parser's answer. 6,518 still resolve to the person the round recorded, none
to anybody else. 301 cannot be re-asked: 264 quote words the page prints under
two different names, 31 are passages a repair has taken out of speech, 5 quote
too little to find, 1 has no words recorded. Two of those 31 are the committee
meeting's pages, which
the check now looks for on their own page before across the sitting; that
changes those two records and no other.

**29 turns checked by a person** against the page images, one a year from
1998 to 2026, drawn fresh: each page shown with the passage marked and the
parser's answer beside it, about four minutes for all 29. All 29 agree.

**Caucus** (`resolve_speakers.py`, `check_bloc_counts.py`). 98.7% of
senators' passages, in the chair or on the floor, carry one: 87.8% confirmed,
9.4% anachronistic, 0.7% disputed, 0.6% undatable, 0.2% inferred across a gap or deduced from the
official count. Set beside the chamber's official count per caucus on four
dates, seven caucus counts differ by one senator. `check_bloc_counts.py`
lists the caucus's senators where the corpus has one too many; where it has
one too few it names nobody, and the 2002 shortfall is a senator missing from
the roster rather than a caucus wrongly given.

## Known issues

- **Three sittings are scans**: 2001-11-21_r72 and 2001-11-29_r74, read by
  OCR and to be excluded from text analysis, and the 1997 tribunal, which
  yields nothing.
- **19 printed labels open a turn with no row.** 6 are in
  the November 2001 scan, and 3 are the secretary's label of 16 June 1999
  printed over an Orden del Día with nothing of his own after it. The other
  10 are labels whose words the page gives only as a note in its own
  paragraph ("— Contenido no inteligible.", a remark made off the
  microphone, "(Lee)" over a document), or as a document or list that is
  nobody's. Whether such a label opens a turn is a question of definition,
  left open rather than decided silently.
- **111 speech passages carry a label that resolves to nobody**, and 45 one
  that fits more than one senator.
- **2,100 senators' passages (1.3%) carry no caucus**: 97 in 1998–1999,
  where no roster survives and the other sources were searched without result,
  and most of the rest in 2014 (578), 2005 (437), 2006 (248) and 2015 (219).
- **5 labels carry a stray parenthesis**: "Sr. PRESIDENTE Menem)", "Sr.
  PRESIDENTE ((Ulloa)", "Sr. PRESIDENTE (Menem) (Menem)" in 1998–1999 and
  "Sr. Presidente)" in 2005, all printed that way, and "Sr. Presidente
  Menem)" in the November 2001 scan. `speaker_raw` keeps
  them as printed; resolution reads them as the label they were meant to be,
  which in this release moved the first of them from Ruckauf, who held the
  office, to Menem, who held the chair, and resolved two more Menem labels
  that had resolved to nobody.
- **Two sittings open on a date a year off their own**, 2008-09-03_r14 and
  2012-08-15_r12: the chamber typed the previous year in the opening note, and
  the corpus keeps what it printed.

## The bundle

`ar_congress_senado_0.5.8`, 1,468 files, 129 MB unpacked, 96 MB as
`ar_congress_senado_0.5.8.tar.gz`. SHA-256 of the archive:
`3342de8c8acafd4428ef1a14bd28f949951ce8d881cc034bffdb378177765aaf`, also in
`ar_congress_senado_0.5.8.tar.gz.sha256` beside it.

It holds the dataset and the code that makes it, laid out as the repository
is: the 817 per-sitting Parquet files, `speakers.parquet`, `parse_stats.csv`,
the archived Senate pages, `reference/senado`, both annotated sets, the
manifest, 23 scripts with `pyproject.toml` and `uv.lock`, the data dictionary,
the licences and `CITATION.cff`. Its `README.md` is `docs/DEPOSIT_README.md`.
New since 0.4.37: `parse_html.py` and the caucus scripts, `reference/gold_html/`
and `data/raw/senado/fichas_archivadas/`. Left out: the Senate's list of
sitting senators with their contact details, which no step reads.
`CHECKSUMS.sha256` covers every
other file; verified on a clean extraction, with the README's loading example
run against it.
