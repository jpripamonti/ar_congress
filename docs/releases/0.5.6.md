# ar_congress 0.5.6

Speaker-attributed corpus of Argentine Senate stenographic transcripts,
February 1998 to September 2026, parsed from the chamber's own PDFs and, for
1998–2003, from its HTML export.

Cut 24 September 2026. PDF parser 0.5.6, HTML parser 0.5.9-html. The previous
release was 0.4.37, of 8 September 2026.

The sitting that does not parse is now the impeachment tribunal of 18
December 1997, a photocopy saved as page images with no text in it. The
no-quorum sitting of 29 November 2001, which 0.4.37 could not parse, is in.

The rise in unattributed passages is the HTML era: its export prints inserted
matter — speeches handed in for the record and never delivered, and the bills
read into it — inside the document, and 83% of those words stand in such a run.

## Changes

`TODO.md`, phases 34 to 41, is the record of each change and the evidence for
it. In summary:

**The HTML era (phase 34).** The portal serves the same URL as a PDF or as
the chamber's HTML export depending on the sitting's age, and the downloader
had been rejecting everything that was not a PDF. Accepting both added 211
sittings of 1998–2003 and 29 of 2025–2026: 559 holdings became 819. A second
parser, `parse_html.py`, reads the exports into the same table, and every row
now says which format it came from (`source_format`; `source_pdf` and
`pdf_sha256` are renamed `source_file` and `source_sha256`). The cover pages
of 1998–2003 were read for who held the chair, and the two senators named
Sapag are told apart by the chamber's courtesy title, which each row settled
that way records in `tiebreak`.

**Caucuses before the rosters.** No roster of caucuses survives before May
2000. The 1998–2000 caucuses are rebuilt from the chair's own calls, which
often name the caucus when giving the floor, from senators' statements on the floor,
from archived copies of senators' own pages, and, where the chamber's official
count per caucus leaves only one answer, from that count. A caucus is carried
across a gap only where the observations on both sides agree, and is marked as
inferred. Every observation records its source.

**Labels the parser was not reading (phases 35 and 41).** The honorific as the
HTML typists spell it ("Sr.Presidente", "Sr Sager", "SR. PRESIDENTE"), labels
split across bold runs, labels ended with a full stop and no dash, labels a
printing defect had cut, and a chair's closing parenthesis left at the start
of the speech. Printed labels that open a turn with no row fell from 91 in
0.4.37 to 19.

**The sitting that held the wrong document (phase 40).** 3–4 September 2014
shipped in 0.4.37 as mostly a committee meeting of 19 August appended to the
file: the parser began reading where that meeting starts. It now reads the
sitting the file is named for, which goes from 118,986 words to 251,293.
Along with it: a file that prints its sitting twice keeps the first copy, a
repeating line counts as a running header only if it opens the page, and a
sitting's body type size may wobble by half a point.

**Notes and italics (phases 39–41).** One row per printed note; "— El texto
es el siguiente:" is the chair's words where the page sets it in the speech
and a note where it stands alone; the secretary's "(Lee:)" is his turn; a
bracketed note printed inside a speaker's sentence is part of that speech.
Italics printed straight after a label now belong to that label — which
returned 154 words of a hearing a senator quoted to the man who asked them.

**New columns.** `convened_as` and `quorum_failed` separate how a sitting was
called from whether it reached quorum, which the chamber's single label
(`session_kind`) conflated. `tiebreak` in `speakers.parquet`, above.

## Verification

Every figure is printed by a script in `scripts/`.

**72 hand-annotated PDF pages** (`eval_gold.py`), up from 36, spanning
1998–2024; the 36 new ones are all before 2016 and were each read twice by
annotators who never saw each other's reading. Boundary and attribution
F1 = 1.000, 310 of 310 turns, on the label alone and with the turn's opening
words. Notes: 82 of 82. A perfect 310 still puts the 95% interval on recall at
0.988 to 1.000. One page was retired, kept with the reason: it was drawn from
the committee meeting the 2014 file appends.

**24 hand-annotated HTML stretches** (`eval_gold_html.py`), new. About 7,000
characters each, cut on the source by offset, four per year across
1998–2003, each read twice; the readings agree on all 195 turn starts, and
both score 195 of 195, notes included.

**The annotations against their sources** (`check_gold.py`): 108 of 108.

**Every sitting against its source file** (`audit_parse.py`): no page apparatus inside a speech
turn outside the three scans, no turn carrying a second speaker's label, no
document yielding more text than it prints (0 of 817), and every one of
14,443 labels of the HTML era's commonest shape — a bold run that begins at
the heading above — attributed to its own speaker. 154,247 passages were
probed against the source and 0.072% could not be located, no sitting above
1% once the scans are set aside. 8 turns of 245,725 open mid-word, and every
one is printed that way.
A median 82.8% of each document's printed text is kept, the rest being
contents pages, attendance rolls and appendices, dropped by design. Every page
of the 90 PDF sittings that keep least was checked against the output: what is
missing before the sitting's close is covers, rolls and contents, not debate.
The audit's split-label check now reads a misspelt office word ("Sr.
Presidented (Maqueda)") the way speaker resolution does; it had been failing
on the one such label whose parse had repaired the typo.

**6,819 turns read blind** in thirteen rounds (`check_blind_reads.py`), up
from 5,463 in nine. 6,529 still resolve to the person the round recorded, none
to anybody else. 290 cannot be re-asked: 264 quote words the page prints under
two different names, 20 are passages a repair has taken out of speech, 6 quote
too little to find. Two of those 20 are the committee meeting's pages, which
the check now looks for on their own page before across the sitting; that
changes those two records and no other.

**29 turns checked by a person** against the page images, one a year from
1998 to 2026, drawn fresh: each page shown with the passage marked and the
parser's answer beside it. All 29 agree.

**Caucus** (`resolve_speakers.py`, `check_bloc_counts.py`). 98.6% of
senators' floor passages carry one: 90.0% confirmed, 7.0% anachronistic, 0.8%
disputed, 0.6% undatable, 0.2% inferred across a gap or deduced from the
official count. Set beside the chamber's official count per caucus on four
dates, seven caucus counts differ by one senator, each listed with the
senators behind it.

## Known issues

- **Three sittings are scans**: 2001-11-21_r72 and 2001-11-29_r74, read by
  OCR and to be excluded from text analysis, and the 1997 tribunal, which
  yields nothing.
- **19 printed labels open a turn with no row.** 6 are the November 2001
  scan; most of the rest are the secretary's label over the document he reads,
  which the page sets as a heading or an insertion. Whether that document is
  his turn is left open.
- **166 speech passages carry a label that resolves to nobody**, and 48 one
  that fits more than one senator.
- **2,120 senators' passages (1.4%) carry no caucus**, most of them 1998–1999,
  where no roster survives and the other sources were searched without result.
- **4 labels carry a stray parenthesis**, all printed that way: "Sr.
  PRESIDENTE Menem)", "Sr. PRESIDENTE ((Ulloa)", "Sr. PRESIDENTE (Menem)
  (Menem)" in 1998–1999 and "Sr. Presidente)" in 2005. `speaker_raw` keeps
  them as printed; resolution reads them as the label they were meant to be,
  which in this release moved the first of them from Ruckauf, who held the
  office, to Menem, who held the chair, and resolved two more Menem labels
  that had resolved to nobody.
- **Two sittings open on a date a year off their own**, 2008-09-03_r14 and
  2012-08-15_r12: the chamber typed the previous year in the opening note, and
  the corpus keeps what it printed.

## The bundle

`ar_congress_senado_0.5.6`, 1,469 files, 129 MB unpacked, 96 MB as
`ar_congress_senado_0.5.6.tar.gz`. SHA-256 of the archive:
`25909a27cf2e7c421736d622ecddb983b2a752e156c93bd36846e043b7b4f2a1`, also in
`ar_congress_senado_0.5.6.tar.gz.sha256` beside it.

It holds the dataset and the code that makes it, laid out as the repository
is: the 817 per-sitting Parquet files, `speakers.parquet`, `parse_stats.csv`,
the archived Senate pages, `reference/senado`, both hand-annotated sets, the
manifest, 23 scripts with `pyproject.toml` and `uv.lock`, the data dictionary,
the licences and `CITATION.cff`. Its `README.md` is `docs/DEPOSIT_README.md`.
New since 0.4.37: `parse_html.py` and the caucus scripts, `reference/gold_html/`
and `data/raw/senado/fichas_archivadas/`. `CHECKSUMS.sha256` covers every
other file; verified on a clean extraction, with the README's loading example
run against it.
